LASSO logistic regression for HER2 status

Exploratory feature selection among HR-positive breast cancer patients. Outcome: HER2-positive (1) versus HER2-negative (0). Sample: 571 patients, 126 positive and 445 negative (22.1% positive).

Source: GitHub commit 31c7670. Both raw clinical files match the earlier cleaning snapshot. The updated EDA performs outcome-dependent drug filtering; that filtering is not reused here.

Feature selection

Seven candidate features were specified before this run: age, sex/gender, race, ethnicity, menopause status, pathologic stage and histology. This diagnostic-profile model does not estimate the future probability of developing breast cancer. Stage and histology require diagnostic workup; availability at a real prediction time must be established.

Receptor measurements and subtype are excluded because they reveal the outcome or define the cohort. Drugs, treatment procedures, follow-up and vital status are excluded because their timing or relation to HER2 could create leakage. IDs, source-site identifiers and duplicate ages are excluded. No univariable p-value screening is used.

Methods

LASSO logistic regression applies an L1 penalty to the coefficients and can set some to zero. Numeric age is median-imputed where needed. Missing categorical values are labeled; categories with fewer than 10 training observations are pooled into an infrequent group. Categorical features use dummy coding with a dropped reference, and all encoded terms are standardized using training data. The intercept is unpenalized; no outcome class weighting or resampling is applied.

Each of five stratified outer folds is evaluated after tuning C on five inner folds by mean log loss. Imputation, pooling, encoding and scaling are fitted inside each training fold. Smaller C means stronger regularization. The 17-value C grid spans 0.0001 to 100. We compare the minimum-loss choice with the strongest penalty within one standard error of minimum loss. The latter is the simpler primary selection rule; the minimum-loss fit is reported as a comparison. Final models are then tuned and fitted on the full sample for feature reporting.

Results

Full-sample minimum-loss C = 0.1; one-standard-error C = 0.1.

The model shows weak discrimination in this first run. The primary nested-CV AUC is 0.514 (approximate conditional 95% interval 0.460 to 0.571). Its Brier score is 0.1720, versus 0.1720 for the prevalence baseline. These results do not establish useful individual prediction.

Minimum-loss fit selects 3 encoded terms from: Histology, Race, Sex/gender. The simpler one-standard-error fit selects 3 terms from: Histology, Race, Sex/gender.

For this snapshot, the selected terms are the male category (8 patients), missing race (82 patients), and mucinous histology (13 patients, none HER2-positive). Thus the race result concerns missingness, not evidence that a particular racial group has higher risk. Both sex/gender and histology terms involve small categories. Their selection is unstable across outer fits. Age, menopause status, stage and ethnicity are zeroed out in this specified fit; this does not establish that they have no association in the population.

Interpretation and limitations

Use the one-standard-error results as the initial parsimonious candidate set. Selection can change with folds, encoding, correlated variables and the penalty rule. An omitted feature is not proven irrelevant, and a selected feature is not proven statistically significant. Individual category levels can be selected without retaining every level of the original variable.

The population excludes unresolved HER2 results, so selection bias is possible. Eight cohort patients have age-source disagreements previously flagged; age_at_diagnosis remains the working source. No nonlinear terms or interactions were considered. Categorical missingness and rare-level pooling are exploratory choices. Calibration-bin summaries are provided for review; external calibration and prospective utility remain unestablished. No post-selection ordinary p-values or confidence intervals are presented.
